[Big River Wildlife Management Area, US-6982](/radio/pota/US-6982/), gave me
exactly the afternoon I had hoped for: a quiet clearing, a little folding
table beside the Jeep, and plenty of people to work on the radio. Only
15-20 minutes from home, too. I had an absolute blast.

The [spot a friend sent me to](https://maps.app.goo.gl/gCvP4p2KiJ7JhFeN8)
was phenomenal. A little way off the road, the clearing had room to spread
out the antenna, with tall grass and the first fall colors around its
edges. It was around 60 degrees, comfortable enough to sit outside and
enjoy it. Only one other car came down while I was there, somebody else
checking out the wildlife management area.

## At a glance

- **Where:** [Big River Wildlife Management Area, US-6982](/radio/pota/US-6982/), Rhode Island, at a clearing beside the access track
- **When:** October 4, 2026; contacts from 1:16 to 2:00 p.m. EDT (17:16-18:00 UTC)
- **Activation:** 31 CW QSOs, all on 20 meters, including two park-to-park contacts
- **Radio:** [Elecraft KX2](/radio/equipment/elecraft-kx2/) driving the [HamGeek Micro PA50-3](/radio/equipment/hamgeek-micro-pa50-3/) amplifier
- **Antenna:** My [20/30/40-meter linked dipole](/notes/2026-09-15-20-30-40-meter-linked-dipole/), deployed as a wide inverted V on a 12-meter fiberglass mast
- **Power:** About 3.4 watts of drive for an indicated 45-46 watts of output

## Giving the tall mast a try

I had brought a few antenna options, including the doublet, but the linked
dipole let me use the amplifier with a resonant antenna. Solar conditions
did not look especially promising before I left. Nearby Reverse Beacon
Network receivers were hearing California and Europe on 20 meters,
though, which was enough encouragement to give that band a try.

Once I arrived, I realized that the 12-meter fiberglass mast was still in
the Jeep. With all that open space, this seemed like a good time to use it.
I set it beside the back of the Jeep, tied it to the vehicle for support,
and put the dipole feedpoint near the top.

A 12-meter mast is nominally about 39 feet tall. I did not measure the
actual feedpoint height, and the mast bowed under the wire, but this was
a satisfyingly high deployment. I estimated the outer ends of the
40-meter extensions at about 15-20 feet above the ground. Roughly 50 feet
of paracord beyond each end let me reach tree tie-offs around 10 feet
high and keep the V nice and wide.

The full wire assembly stayed up, with the links open for 20 meters. The
ends of the active 20-meter sections were higher up the slope than those
outer wire ends. I did not measure those heights either; the useful
observation was how much room this clearing gave me to raise and spread
out the whole antenna.

## Forty-five watts and some lovely DX

The Micro PA50 was happy with the dipole. With about 3.4 watts from the
KX2, its display showed roughly 45-46 watts out and an antenna-side SWR
of about 1.2:1. The KX2 indicated about 1.4:1 looking into the amplifier.
Everything was working comfortably, and I could settle in at the table
and enjoy the radio.

I stayed on 20 meters for the whole activation and made 31 CW contacts
in about 44 minutes. Germany and England both made it into the log,
along with France and Hungary. The two California contacts felt like
DX too: about 2,500 miles from Rhode Island is a substantial trip for
a signal from a little table in the woods.

The combination of the high dipole, the modest amplifier, and a
comfortable place to operate worked beautifully for this outing. I
would happily bring the same setup back here. There is something
particularly satisfying about a homebrew antenna doing exactly what
I built it to do.
