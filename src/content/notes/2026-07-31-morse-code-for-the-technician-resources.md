the [2026 Northeast HamXposition Technician
Toolkit](https://hamxposition.org/2026/08/08/technician-toolkit-at-2026-hamx/)
on Friday, August 14. The central idea is simple: learn characters as sounds,
build a short practice habit, and make the first contact small and safe.

## Know what a Technician can already do

The [ARRL chart of U.S. Technician
privileges](https://www.arrl.org/files/file/Tech%20Band%20Chart/Tech%20Band%20Chart.pdf)
shows the CW windows on 80, 40, 15, and 10 meters and the 200-watt PEP limit
that applies there. [FCC Part 97](https://www.arrl.org/part-97-text) remains the
underlying rules text. The talk is about making those privileges feel usable,
not about memorizing band edges from a slide.

## Choose a learning path

- [Long Island CW Club](https://longislandcwclub.org/) offers ongoing live
  classes at multiple levels.
- [CWops CW Academy](https://cwa.cwops.org/) offers coached, session-based
  classes from beginner through advanced levels.
- [CW Innovations](https://cwinnovations.net/) focuses on conversational CW,
  head copy, and confidence on the air.

You do not need all three. Pick the structure and schedule you are most likely
to keep using.

## Practice that fits your day

- [Morse Mania](https://morse.countrymania.net/) provides focused, interactive
  character practice on
  [iPhone and iPad](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/morse-mania-learn-morse-code/id1511042196)
  and [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=net.countrymania.morse).
- [Learn CW](https://learncw.vailmorse.com/) provides similar interactive
  character practice directly in a web browser.
- [Morse Code Ninja](https://www.morsecode.ninja/learn/) provides incremental
  audio, podcast, and video practice. Its audio lessons fit especially well
  into a commute or walk.
- [Vail Morse](https://vailmorse.com/) combines a browser decoder with an
  internet repeater. The optional [Vail Adapter](https://vailadapter.com/)
  connects a physical key or paddle by USB.
- [W1AW code-practice files](https://www.arrl.org/code-practice-files) provide
  regularly updated copy at several speeds.
- [ARRL operating aids](https://www.arrl.org/operating-aids) collect common Q
  signals, abbreviations, state abbreviations, and procedural shorthand.
- [My CW Practice Schedule](/radio/cw-practice/) finds the next SST, MST, and
  CWT sessions in local time and walks through each exchange.
- [My CW QSO Walkthrough](/radio/cw-qso/) provides interactive prompts for a
  standard contact, with copy fields and local ADIF export.

Start with one sound-based tool. Use a normal character speed with extra space
between characters, rather than slowing each character until you can count its
parts. [Morse Code Ninja's reference](https://morsecode.ninja/reference/)
explains the Farnsworth approach; its [beginner
advice](https://morsecode.ninja/advice/) also covers the counting trap and the
move toward words and useful exchanges.

## Get on the air without making the first contact enormous

- [Parks on the Air](https://parksontheair.com/) gives a new operator a short,
  repeated, and predictable exchange to listen for. The [official getting
  started guide](https://docs.pota.app/) begins with hunting, and the
  [POTA spot page](https://pota.app/) shows who is currently on the air.
- No HF receiver yet? The public [WebSDR](http://www.websdr.org/) and
  [KiwiSDR](http://kiwisdr.com/public/) directories list internet-connected
  receivers you can tune in a browser. Take a frequency from the POTA spot
  page, choose a receiver that covers that band, select CW, tune near the
  listed frequency, and confirm the callsign you hear on the air.
- The [POTA Hunter Guide](https://docs.pota.app/docs/hunter_guide-english.html)
  explains listening, calling, exchanges, spotting, and hunter credit.
- The official [POTA CW Guide](https://docs.pota.app/docs/cw_guide.html) covers
  common CW exchanges from both sides of a park contact.
- [`potacw.guide` — CW POTA: A Field Manual](https://potacw.guide/) walks
  through the exchange line by line and includes a customizable activator and
  hunter cheat sheet.
- [My first all-CW POTA activation](https://qrper.com/2026/04/n1rwj-my-first-cw-pota-activation-dont-wait-to-try-it/)
  is the field report behind the “do not wait” story in the talk.

Hunt before you activate. Listen through a few contacts, recognize your own
call, learn `AGN?` and `QRS PSE`, and arrange a patient first QSO if that makes
the step feel safer. POTA can be the gateway; it does not have to be the
destination.

## See the five-part station

The complete portable station in the talk is a radio, key or paddle, antenna,
power, and a log. My [radio equipment catalog](/radio/equipment/) and [packed
station overview](/radio/shack/) show how those systems fit together in the
stations I actually carry.

- **Radio:** the [QRP Labs QMX](/radio/equipment/qrp-labs-qmx/), [Elecraft
  KX2](/radio/equipment/elecraft-kx2/), and [Elecraft
  KH1](/radio/equipment/elecraft-kh1/) illustrate different portable-CW
  tradeoffs rather than a required shopping list.
- **Key:** the [K8CES Zippy paddle](/radio/equipment/k8ces-zippy-paddle/) is the
  small paddle I use in the demonstration. My [photo-by-photo assembly
  guide](/notes/2026-07-29-building-k8ces-zippy-paddle/) documents the giveaway
  batch.
- **Antenna and the rest of the station:** the [Pulaski State Park doublet
  test](/notes/2026-08-08-pulaski-state-park-doublet-test/) shows a wire,
  battery, paddle, radio, and log together at a picnic table. The [Sachuest
  Point activation](/notes/2026-06-04-sachuest-point-national-wildlife-refuge-pota/)
  shows the compact alternative: KX2, internal battery, clip-on paddle,
  headphones, and a portable vertical carried down a trail.
- **Log:** [Ham2K Portable Logger](https://polo.ham2k.com/) is the phone logger
  mentioned in the talk; its [first-operation
  guide](https://polo.ham2k.com/docs/first-operation/) covers creating an
  operation, logging contacts, and exporting ADIF.

Borrow before buying when possible. A few contacts reveal whether weight,
built-in tuning, a large display, or a very fast setup matters to the way you
actually operate.
