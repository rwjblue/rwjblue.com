Joe, [KA5I](https://www.qrz.com/db/KA5I), and I met through the
[Everyday Ham Discord](https://www.everydayham.com/connect/). While we were
chatting there, he noticed I was in Rhode Island and mentioned that work brings
him here a couple of times each year. When this trip came together, we made
plans to get together and play radio while he was in town.

I picked him up in Warwick and we headed to
[Rocky Point State Park, US-2879](/radio/pota/US-2879/), where an open pavilion
gave us a picnic table and a clear patch of grass for the antenna. By the end
of the evening Joe had completed his first park activation, and my log held 38
SSB contacts.

The radio was only part of the evening. Joe and I have similar stories: family
and kids pulled both of us away from the hobby for a while, and now we are
finding our way back into it. We spent at least as much time comparing
portable-radio ideas and swapping stories as we did calling CQ.

## At a glance

- **Where:** [Rocky Point State Park, US-2879](/radio/pota/US-2879/), Warwick, Rhode Island; an open pavilion roughly 100 meters from the shoreline
- **When:** August 5, 2026, 22:24 to 23:40 UTC (6:24 to 7:40 PM EDT)
- **Activation:** 38 SSB QSOs in my log, all on 20 meters, including 4 park-to-parks; Joe completed his first POTA activation as KA5I
- **Radios:** [Yaesu FT-891](/radio/equipment/yaesu-ft-891/) at 75 and 20 watts, followed by the [Elecraft KX2](/radio/equipment/elecraft-kx2/) at 10 watts
- **Antenna:** [KJ6ER PERformer](/radio/equipment/kj6er-performer/) configured for 20 meters
- **Power:** [12 Ah Bioenno LiFePO4 battery](/radio/equipment/bioenno-12ah/), then a [3 Ah Bioenno](/radio/equipment/bioenno-3ah/), then the KX2 internal battery
- **Logging:** Ham2K Portable Logger
- **Conditions:** Sunny and about 75 degrees at the start, with a light breeze and a beautiful sunset over Narragansett Bay

## A pavilion with a water view

Rocky Point turned out to be an excellent evening operating site. The pavilion
was open, the picnic table gave both of us plenty of room, and the antenna went
into the grass just outside. We were roughly 100 meters from the shoreline,
with the water clearly visible beyond the lawn. There were also no nearby power
lines competing with the setup.

I configured the PERformer for 20 meters and arranged its elevated radials in
opposite directions along one straight line. The goal was to keep the pattern
as close to omnidirectional as practical instead of trying to favor one region.
The antenna tuned to about 1.1:1 in the lower part of the 20-meter SSB segment
and behaved well across the part of the band we used.

Joe has held his license for a while, but he is just getting back into radio and
is still fairly new to operating HF. He is interested in learning CW but is not
yet comfortable enough to use it for an activation, so we kept the entire
evening on voice and passed the microphone back and forth. I used the full-size
Elecraft hand microphone instead of my smaller
[G7UFO Turret mini microphone](/radio/equipment/g7ufo-turret-mini-microphone/).
The larger mic's normal PTT button was much easier to hand across the table,
and the audio reports were consistently good.

Joe has been considering a KX2 for work travel, so the evening gave him a chance
to operate mine and see how much radio fits into that little package.

## Three batteries, two radios, and one unresolved problem

We started with the FT-891 set to 75 watts and the 12 Ah Bioenno battery that I
believed was fully charged. Almost immediately, the voltage fell to 10.4 V
under load and the radio shut down. I reduced the FT-891 to 20 watts, but the
battery still cut out as soon as we keyed up.

This was not the pack's first strange field failure. At Tallman Mountain during
my [Rhode Island-to-Florida rove](/notes/2026-06-19-rhode-island-to-florida-rove-day-one/),
the same battery would not power the FT-891 at all even though its charger had
shown green. It later measured fully charged and worked again. Seeing another
failure under transmit load means I need to test the pack, its BMS, and the
connectors properly before trusting it on another activation.

The backup was the little 3 Ah Bioenno I normally pair with the KX2. It ran the
FT-891 at 20 watts surprisingly well for all 22 contacts at that power before
it, too, was exhausted. We still were not ready to stop, so I switched to the
KX2, its freshly charged internal battery, and 10 watts.

We made the opening two contacts at 75 watts, followed by 22 at 20 watts and the
final 14 at 10 watts.

## Ten watts was enough for Germany

The lower power did not make the activation feel small. At 20 watts we worked
[UA3G](https://www.qrz.com/db/UA3G) in European Russia, about 4,700 miles away.
He told us he was running 400 watts into a three-element Yagi. That same stretch
included [W6CEP](https://www.qrz.com/db/W6CEP) in Long Beach, California, about
2,600 miles away, and [AB6GS](https://www.qrz.com/db/AB6GS) in Hulett, Wyoming,
about 1,700 miles away. We also reached Montana, Idaho, and a long run of
stations through Florida, Alabama, and the Midwest.

With the KX2 at 10 watts, [DG4DW](https://www.qrz.com/db/DG4DW) in Germany came
back with a 55 report from about 3,600 miles away. He was using 100 watts and a
seven-element Yagi, but the path still made for a great demonstration of what a
compact travel station can do. The final 14 contacts were at 10 watts,
including stations in Alabama, Georgia, Illinois, and Indiana.

We also added four park-to-park contacts along the way.

## The part worth repeating

The weather could hardly have been better. A light breeze moved through the
pavilion while the evening light crossed the lawn and water. Bugs only started
to become noticeable as we packed up, by which point we had already had exactly
the sort of activation we wanted.

Joe expects to be back in Rhode Island a couple of times each year. I hope this
was only our first activation together. Getting him through his first POTA
activation was satisfying, but an evening at a picnic table talking radio with
another ham was the real success.

## What worked

- The open pavilion gave us a comfortable shared operating position with a
  picnic table, nearby grass for the antenna, and a view of the bay.
- The 20-meter PERformer tuned easily and produced strong reports at every
  power level we tried.
- The 3 Ah Bioenno and the KX2 internal battery kept the activation going after
  the primary battery failed.
- The full-size hand microphone was the right choice for two operators passing
  control of the station back and forth.
- The KX2 at 10 watts completed 14 SSB contacts and reached Germany.

## To adjust next time

- Load-test the 12 Ah Bioenno battery, BMS, cabling, and connectors before it
  returns to the field kit.
- Bring the [50 Ah LiTime battery](/radio/equipment/litime-50ah/) when the plan
  involves extended FT-891 SSB operation.
- Keep the 3 Ah Bioenno in the bag; it carried the 20-watt FT-891 much farther
  than I expected.
