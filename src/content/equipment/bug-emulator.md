This is the electronic trainer that let me start learning bug sending before I
owned a [mechanical bug](/radio/equipment/mechanical-bug/). It preserves the
manual dah side of the technique while generating mechanical-style dits, but
its speed can be reduced to roughly 10 WPM without the delicate adjustments or
extra pendulum weights a physical bug would need. Bob's
[detailed introduction on QRP'er](https://qrper.com/2025/09/for-bug-fists-on-the-go-the-don-lawson-wb9cyy-bug-emulator/)
explains that distinction and the design's portable use cases.

I discovered the emulator through
[episode 258 of the Ham Radio Workbench podcast](https://workbench.libsyn.com/hrwb-258-morse-code-bug-emulator-with-don-wb9cyy-and-bob-wo6w),
in which Don Lawson WB9CYY and Bob Leichner WO6W explain its development and why
a conventional keyer's bug mode does not feel quite like a real bug.

After listening, I emailed Bob to say I was interested in getting an emulator.
He called me while I was driving home during the 2026 New England QSO Party
weekend, and we talked for a good long while about the emulator and learning to
send with it. What stood out was how genuinely happy he was to share what he
knew. It was pretty awesome to have that kind of personal help from someone who
had worked on the device.

The emulator is compact enough for a field bag and runs for more than 200 hours
on a coin cell. Its adjustable low speed makes it forgiving for early practice,
while its timing still allows an individual bug fist to develop instead of
correcting every dit into keyer-perfect code.
